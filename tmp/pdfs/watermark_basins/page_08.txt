Effect of the distillation temperature.What happens when the adversary samples its distillation traces
at a temperature the verifier does not know? We generate the teacher’s traces atT=1.0,0.7, and0.5
and distill100students from each setting. A lowerTconcentrates the teacher’s output distribution on
its most likely tokens. For WM-Basin, the reference gradient bank remains fixed atT=1.0, creating
a mismatch at the two lower temperatures. Tab. 2 shows that lowering the distillation temperature
costs the black-box baseline most of its power at every window, whereas WM-Basin atp≤0.01still flags
every suspect atn-gram=1and about half of them atn-gram=3, even atT=0.5. Temperature thus
governswhat the tokens reveal, not what the gradient reveals: lowering it is a cheap evasion against
the baseline but not against WM-Basin.
Effect of the teacher model.Are some teachers harder to protect than others? We distill the same
Qwen2.5-3B student from each of four teachers spanning three families and8B to120B parameters:
DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B, gpt-oss-120B (Agarwal et al., 2025), QwQ-32B (Team, 2025), and
Llama-3.1-8B (Grattafiori et al., 2024), rebuilding the reference bank for each. Tab. 3 shows that, at
p≤0.05, WM-Basin detects96to100%of distilled models for every teacher. The baseline loses most
of its power on two teachers and approaches WM-Basin on the other two. Its power is thus inherited
from the teacher and varies sharply with it, whereas WM-Basin is robust across teachers; App. C.2
relates this to how often each student repeats its contexts.
