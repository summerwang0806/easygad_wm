3.3 Post-processing and Basin Dynamics
In §2.2, we showed that a watermarked student sits in a narrow basin: a small step along the watermark
direction(Eq.(1))leavesit, whileamuchlargerstepalongthetaskdirectiondoesnot. Wenowexamine
in which direction standard post-processing moves the student, and whether it removes the watermark.
Effect of model compression.Can an adversary quantize or prune the watermark out of a deployed
student? We apply int8, int4, or nf4 quantization, or10to30%unstructured pruning, to the same
100suspects. Tab. 6 shows that only int8 and10%pruning leave a usable model, and both cost
WM-Basin at most three points at any window and operating point. The other operations weaken
detection but also leave the student unusable. The weight-space decomposition explains why (Tab. 16):
every operation displaces the student at least three orders of magnitude further orthogonally to the
watermark direction than along it. Compression therefore does not move the student out of the
watermark basin, and it remains detectable.
Effect of continued SFT and RL.Does continued training on clean data move the distilled model out
of its watermark basin? We continue training the suspects via SFT on cross-domain data and via
GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) on verifiable tasks (details in App. D.2), holding the probe and the reference
bank fixed. Tab. 7 shows that continued RL leaves the student detectable while continued SFT weakens
the signal: WM-Basin detects every distilled model in every GRPO arm atp≤0.05and every window,
whereas both SFT arms substantially reduce its power. This ordering matches how far each update
moves the model along the watermark direction: SFT on general reasoning traces moves it furthest,
SFT on multilingual math less, and GRPO barely at all. The open-weight detector, teacher-forced on
thedistillationtraces, neverthelessstilldetectstheSFT-trainedstudents. ContinuedSFTthusweakens
the signal observable in the student’s outputs without moving the model out of the watermark basin.
