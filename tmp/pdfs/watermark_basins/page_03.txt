Our contributions are as follows:
•Discovery of watermark basins.We find that watermarked distillation places every distilled student
in a persistent basin, spanned by the ordinary task direction and a faint, key-specific watermark
direction. Leaving this basin without knowledge of the key is difficult: post-training operations
(quantization, pruning, supervised fine-tuning, RL) displace weights primarily along the task axis
and leave the key-specific component intact, which we exploit for detection.
•A parameter-space black-box detector (WM-Basin).WM-Basin looks for distillation evidence in pa-
rameter space. It maps text outputs to gradients of a fixed clean probe model, removes the shared
task subspace, and scores the residual alignment against decoy keys. The watermark becomes far
more detectable than in token space, with exact finite-sample false-positive control and no access to
the suspect’s parameters.
•Robust detection at scale.Across24student architectures (135M to8.2B parameters),4teachers,
and4watermarking schemes, WM-Basin achieves79%TPR at0.1%FPR versus29%for the state-of-
the-art token-level detector, and remains effective under quantization, pruning, and RL post-training.
2 Watermark-Basin Detection
2.1 Threat Model and Statistical Setup
Distillation setting and adversary model.A proprietary teacher modelM teacher serves completions
watermarked under a secret keyk⋆ ←KeyGen, drawn from the watermark scheme’s key-generation
procedure. Following deployed systems (Aaronson, 2022; Dathathri et al., 2024; Sander et al., 2026;
Anthropic, 2026b), we consider distortion-free schemes: the teacher’s marginal next-token distribution
is preserved, but for each promptxthe outputy∼ Mteacher(x;k ⋆)is coupled to the pseudorandomness
induced byk ⋆. The adversary adaptively choosesNpromptsX train ={x i}Ni=1, receives watermarked
traces{y i}, buildsD={(x i, yi)}, and trains a studentMsus by supervised fine-tuning onD. With-
out knowledge ofk ⋆, watermarked completions are indistinguishable from clean text, so the default
adversary is unaware of the watermark and uses the traces as-is. After fine-tuning, the adversary may
further post-trainM sus on clean data, e.g., via continued SFT or GRPO (Guo et al., 2025).
Verifier regime.An audit is opened by the verifier, either the model owner or an authorized third party,
and begins by naming one capability the owner sells and the suspect appears to compete on. We call it
theaudited domainD(e.g., math) and letP D be its induced prompt distribution; the verifier chooses
both and draws every prompt it uses fromPD. It holds two disjoint pools: abuildpoolX build used to
construct the detector, and atestpoolX test used to read the suspect. Neither overlaps the adversary’s
prompts, which the verifier never sees, and keeping build and test prompts apart avoids score inflation
from prompt reuse. The verifier’s access toMsus isblack-box: it observes only texts sampled on the
test prompts. The access is alsounsupervisedin the sense of Sander et al. (2024): the verifier does not
hold the tracesMsus was trained on, only fresh teacher traces on the build prompts (unwatermarked,
watermarked underk ⋆, and watermarked underMindependent decoy keysk j ←KeyGen), together
with a pool of public clean checkpoints. It knows the system prompts used to queryMsus and the
teacher, because it chooses them, and the number of test prompts sent to the suspect is the query
budget.
