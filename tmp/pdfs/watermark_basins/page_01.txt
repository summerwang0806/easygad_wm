where an adversary trains a student model on the teacher model’s outputs. To obtain evidence
of distillation, model providers can watermark the teacher’s outputs, aiming to leave a radioac-
tive signal in the student’s outputs. Existing black-box detectors search for such radioactivity
using token-level statistics of the suspect model’s outputs. However, token-level signals are not
always detectable, for instance when the distillation traces are sampled at a low temperature.
In this work, we discover thewatermark basin, a persistent signal that enables more reliable
watermark detection: during fine-tuning, watermarked teacher traces embed a distinct, key-
specificgeometric signal in parameter spacethat lies orthogonal to the ordinary task-specific
fine-tuning subspace. Building on this, we introduce Watermark Basin Alignment (WM-Basin),
a black-box watermark detector that queries the suspect model through its API, without ac-
cess to its weights or logits. WM-Basin maps generated text to gradient representations using
a fixed clean probe model, projects out the ordinary fine-tuning task directions, and tests
whether the residual gradient aligns with the watermark basin induced by the watermark key.
We evaluate WM-Basin extensively across diverse teacher models and student architectures
(from 135M to 8.2B parameters) distilled on mathematical reasoning. At a strict 0.1% false-
positive rate, WM-Basin detects 79% of distilled models atn-gram=1and 45% atn-gram=3,
substantially improving over the state-of-the-art token-level baseline (29% and 1.3%, respec-
tively). Because the watermark signal resides in a persistent basin, it survives post-distillation
modifications including quantization, pruning, and reinforcement-learning post-training.
