Capability also transfers through synthetic instructions, model-generated reasoning, rubric or reward

high-quality reasoning sets sharply improve students (Muennighoff et al., 2025).
Defenses against extraction.Existing defenses restrict what the API returns, monitor extraction-like
querying (Anthropic, 2026a; Google Threat Intelligence Group, 2026), or make outputs less useful for
training via antidistillation sampling or trace rewriting (Savani et al., 2026; Ma et al., 2026). Such
methods trade away utility for benign users or assume that the attacker stays observable during
collection. That assumption is fragile in practice: adversaries spread collection across many accounts
and third-party API resellers, so no single query stream looks like extraction (Anthropic, 2026a).
Detection is complementary: it acts after the fact and leaves the deployed model untouched. A
parallel black-box line, dataset inference (Maini et al., 2021), resolves ownership by testing whether a
suspect shares private knowledge of the victim’s data, without inserting a watermark; it observes that
inserted watermarks are off-distribution and therefore not always preserved under stealing. Classical
model watermarking instead hides a signal directly in the weights or via backdoors (Uchida et al.,
2017; Adi et al., 2018), read from the parameters or by querying a trigger set; both assume the owner
watermarked the suspect model under test, whereas our suspect is a model the owner never trained.
Watermarking and radioactivity.Text watermarking embeds a secret statistical signal into generated
text (Kirchenbauer et al., 2023; Kuditipudi et al., 2023; Sander et al., 2026). The idea of tracing
data through a trained model originates in computer vision: Sablayrolles et al. (2020) show that
imperceptible “radioactive” watermarks in a dataset leave a statistical residue in models trained on it,
detectable with a calibratedp-value even when only1%of the data is marked. Our rank test follows
the same framework. Sander et al. (2024) carry radioactivity over to language models, showing that
watermarked distillation leaves a key-specific residue in the student, and others study how well such
watermarks survive distillation (Sander et al., 2026; Zhao et al., 2022; Pan et al., 2025). The black-box
verifier of Sander et al. (2024), which counts watermark-favored tokens in a suspect’s sampled text,
