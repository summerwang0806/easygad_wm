C.6 Probe Model and Warm-Up
The probe model turns text into a gradient signal. In this section, we study the impact of the probe
model on the performance of WM-Basin.
Choice of probe model.Does the verifier need a probe model from the suspect’s own family? Table 14
varies only the probeθprobe that §3.1 holds at a clean Qwen2.5-3B base, across ten checkpoints from
0.5B to8B spanning four Qwen2.5 scales or post-training states, three Llama-3 checkpoints, and two
families whose tokenizers sit farthest from the student’s152k vocabulary. Every probe receives the
same warm-up, and because each defines a different gradient coordinate space its reference bank is
rebuilt with the default recipe; the suspect cohort, clean PCA pool and threen-gram windows stay
fixed. Family costs the detector nothing: every probe recovers all suspects atn-gram=1, and atn-
gram=3and0.1%FPR the three Llama-3 probes land inside or just under the band the Qwen probes
span, despite sharing no tokenizer with the student. The loss instead tracks how far the probe’s
vocabulary sits from the student’s, the smallest vocabulary losing most while still staying an order
of magnitude above the baseline in the same cell, and falling further behind the Qwen probes as the
threshold tightens atn-gram=2and3. The verifier can therefore audit with whatever checkpoint it
holds, preferring a nearby vocabulary.
Impact of warm-up.The probe warm-up of §3.1 is one short LoRA pass on clean teacher traces, run
before any key gradient is taken; Tab. 15 sweeps it one knob at a time from the default, in a single
scoring pass per arm. We reportn-gram=3because it is the only window that separates the arms: at
n-gram=1every probe in Tab. 14 already reads100at all three levels.
Removing the warm-up costs most of the detector’s power at0.1%FPR: an untrained, randomly
initialised adapter keeps about a third of the default’s TPR there, and the bare base model almost
none. Gradients are taken with respect to the adapter parametersθprobe (§2.3), so a random adapter
still defines a usable coordinate space and retains real signal at5%FPR, whereas the bare model has
no adapter to differentiate against. The warm-up therefore sharpens a signal the probe already carries
rather than creating one.
Warming on prompts from different domains and datasets, i.e., chat, MMLU, GSM8K or MATH
instead of the audited OpenR1-Math distribution costs11to21points at0.1%FPR and at most
one key at5%. Code is the single exception (37%), and it is the warm-up corpus farthest from the
reasoning traces the reference bank is built from. This is a separate question from where the verifier
draws itsdetectionprompts, which do have to come from the audited domain or a similar one (§3.2).
