Effect of the watermark scheme.Is detection tied to the watermark the teacher happens to deploy? We
replace TextSeal in the teacher’s decoder with GumbelMax (Aaronson, 2022), KGW (γ=0.5) (Kirchen-
bauer et al., 2023), and SynthID (Dathathri et al., 2024), rebuilding the reference bank for each and
distilling50students per scheme and window. Tab. 4 shows that all three transfer: WM-Basin flags
every student atp≤0.01andn-gram=1under each scheme, and it outperforms the baseline in every
cell, by a margin that widens with the window atp≤0.05. WM-Basin needs only traces the teacher
generated under a key, not the watermark’s own detection test. The baseline, in contrast, scores the
suspect’s tokens with that test, which must be derived scheme by scheme (App. B.4). To audit a
new keyed watermark, the verifier therefore only generates teacher traces under its keys, rebuilds the
reference bank, and runs the same detector.

Effect of the audited domain and system prompt.How close must the audited domain be to the
adversary’s distillation data? We hold the100suspects fixed and change the domain the verifier
audits, from the math domain they were distilled on (OpenR1-Math, MATH, GSM8K) to two domains
outside it (code and chat). For each domain we rebuild the probe warm-up, the task subspace, and
both detectors from that domain’s own prompts, none of them the teacher’s training system prompt;
App. C.4 lists the exact prompts. Tab. 5 shows that WM-Basin’s detection power follows where the
suspects were distilled: it ranks every math domain above both non-math ones in every cell, whereas
the baseline ranks code above GSM8K atn-gram=1and2. A hit therefore tells the verifier on which
domain the suspect’s text carries the watermark, not only that it carries one; a low score elsewhere
means nothing was found there, not that the model is clean.
