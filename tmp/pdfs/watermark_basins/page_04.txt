2.2 Watermark Basins in Parameter Space
We first use a white-box setting toinvestigatewhat distinguishes a student trained on watermarked
traces from one trained on clean traces, and thus what makes a model radioactive. Fig. 1 presented the
watermark basin; here we test that geometry directly with access to the student weights. We fix the
true keyk ⋆ and fine-tune the same base model either on teacher traces watermarked withk⋆, giving
θstudent, or on unwatermarked traces, giving a clean controlθctrl. The teacher, prompts, initialization,
and training recipe are otherwise identical, so both students learn the same task while only one sees
the watermark. Let∆ total :=θ student −θ base be the total update from watermarked training and
∆task :=θ ctrl −θ base the clean task update. We isolate the watermark-specific part of the update by
removing from∆ total its component along∆task:
∆wm := ∆total − ⟨∆total,∆ task⟩∥∆task∥22 ∆task.(1)
The subtracted term is the part of the watermarked update that the clean student also makes, i.e.,
learning the task. What remains,∆wm, is the part that only the watermark can explain. We normalize
∆wm and∆ task to obtain the watermark axisˆ∆wm and the task axisˆ∆task.
To map the basin, we start atθstudent and perturb the weights along each axis,θstudent +t wm ˆ∆wm +
ttask ˆ∆task, over a grid of step sizes(twm, ttask). At each point, we apply the open-weight detector of
Sander et al. (2024) to the perturbed student and record−log10 p, where a smallerp-value is stronger
evidence of distillation. Fig. 3 shows that the surface is strongly anisotropic: a small step along the
watermark axis makes the watermark signal collapse, whereas much larger displacements along the
task axis leave it detectable. The basin is broad alongˆ∆task but has a sharp boundary alongˆ∆wm.
This asymmetry gives the two axes different roles. The width alongˆ∆task suggests why ordinary post-
training can alter task behavior without erasing the basin, a hypothesis we test under compression and
continued training in §3.3. The narrowness alongˆ∆wm makes the basin key-specific, which motivates
comparing against decoy keys rather than thresholding a generic watermark score. This analysis relies
on access to the student’s parameters, which a black-box verifier does not have. Next, we show how
to detect the same key-specific signal using only text sampled from the suspect.
