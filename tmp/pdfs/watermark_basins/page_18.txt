selects reference traces; the probe, the random projection, the build prompts, and the fittedUare
identical for allM+1candidates. The claimed key is therefore equally likely to occupy any of the
M+1ranks, assuming no ties. Thresholding its rankp-value then controls the FPR in finite samples:
the FPR is at mostαfor any threshold, and equalsαwhenαlies on the attainable rank grid. The≥
in Eq. (4) counts ties against rejection, so ties cost power rather than validity. None of this needs a
parametric model ofS(Pesarin and Salmaso, 2010): Eq. (4) places no restriction on the score, so the
guarantee survives arbitrary non-linear transformations of the suspect model. App. B.6 measures the
realized rate against the nominal one for every deployed key.
The smallest attainablep-value is1/(M+1), so the decoy count sets the finest significance an audit
can certify: reporting atα=0.001requiresM≥999decoys. Decoy scoring is offline and reuses the
build-once artefacts, so improving the resolution can be cheap in practice.
